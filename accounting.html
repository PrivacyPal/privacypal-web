The consent problem

Your client never consented to that paste

Section 7216 makes it a criminal matter for a return preparer to disclose or use tax return information without the taxpayer's written consent. A staff accountant dropping a K-1 into a public chatbot is a disclosure: a misdemeanor carrying up to $1,000 and a year, plus $250 per disclosure under Section 6713. The AICPA's Confidential Client Information Rule says the same thing in professional terms, and the FTC Safeguards Rule already requires a written plan naming who may touch client data and where it goes.

Nobody in your firm is trying to breach any of that. They are trying to finish a return at 9pm in March. PrivacyPal makes the compliant path the default path: staff keep the AI, client identity never crosses the perimeter, and every prompt lands in a log your peer reviewer, your insurer and your clients can be shown.
