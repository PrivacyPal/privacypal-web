Module 01 of 09

The shift, the market and the wedge

Before you can sell PrivacyPal you have to explain, in ninety seconds, why a competent security leader is blocking the most valuable productivity tool their company has ever had. That explanation is the sale.

TL;DR
  • Every company is now running AI whether or not it approved AI. The question is not adoption, it is governance.
  • Security teams have three bad options today: ban it, write a policy nobody reads, or look away.
  • Every governance vendor sits somewhere on a mechanism ladder. Every rung above "coach" costs the buyer accuracy. Only one mechanism, substitution, costs nothing.
  • Our wedge is not fear. It is turning the person who blocks AI into the person who ships it.
  • You never lead with the product. You lead with the tradeoff the buyer is already losing.

1. What actually changed

Ten years of data-loss prevention was built on a simple assumption: sensitive data leaves the company as a file, through a channel you can inspect. Email attachments. USB drives. Uploads to a known SaaS host. DLP watched the channels.

Generative AI broke that assumption in two ways at once.

  1. The payload became a prompt. The leak is now free text typed by a human into a text box, mixed with context, mid-thought. No file, no classification label, no filename to match.
  2. The channel became every channel. A browser tab, a desktop app, an IDE, a coding agent running on a laptop, an MCP server calling internal tools, an agent talking to another agent. Some of that traffic never touches the corporate network at all.

So the security leader is looking at a category of exposure that their existing stack was structurally not built to see. That is the real emotional state you are walking into. Not "we do not care about AI." It is "we cannot see it, so we said no, and now the business hates us."

4. The mechanism ladder

This is the single most useful idea in your entire toolkit, and almost nobody in the market articulates it. Every AI governance product in existence does one of six things to a risky prompt. Protection with no accuracy tax

Walk a technical buyer up this ladder and let them place their current tooling on it themselves. They will land on rung 3 or 4, and they already know rung 3 and 4 are why nobody uses the tool. You do not have to attack a competitor. The ladder does it.

The distinction that wins the technical argument

Tokenization preserves referential integrity. Substitution preserves meaning. If you replace "Acme Corp" with ENTITY_7741 every time, the model can tell that the same thing is being discussed. It cannot tell that the thing is a company, in an industry, of a size, in a jurisdiction. Ask a model to draft a credit memo about ENTITY_7741 earning NUM_0032 and you get sludge. Ask it about a mid-market industrial distributor earning $14.2M and you get a credit memo. That is the accuracy tax, and it is why redaction-era tools sit unused after the pilot.

Module 02 of 09

The mechanism: Privacy Twins on device

PrivacyPal is a mechanism-first company. You do not get to make a claim without naming the machinery that makes it true. This module is the machinery.

TL;DR
  • Privacy Twins are statistically accurate synthetic values substituted for real ones before a prompt leaves the device, and reverted in the response.
  • The whole pipeline runs on the endpoint. PrivacyPal never sees unencrypted customer data. That is what "zero-knowledge" means here.
  • Five stages: detect, classify, synthesize, map, revert.
  • You must know the internals. You must also know that you do not say the internals to a prospect. Answer plus mechanism, then stop.

1. The journey of one prompt

One prompt, start to finish. Everything inside the panel happens on the endpoint.

2. The five stages

StageWhat happens
1 · DetectionMulti-layer sensitive-data detection: named entity recognition, regular expressions, and component splitting (so a full name, a street address or an account number is broken into its parts).
2 · ClassificationThe detected entity is categorized. This is what drives which kind of twin gets generated.
3 · SynthesisA statistically faithful synthetic value is generated for that entity type. The generation is model-based, not a random string.
4 · MappingThe real-to-twin mapping is stored in the encrypted vault and graph. This is the only place the relationship exists, and it never leaves the boundary.
5 · ReversionOn the response, twins are swapped back to real values. Multi-turn conversations stay coherent through continuation IDs.

Reversion runs in five passes

Reversion is the part that competitors get wrong, so it is worth knowing precisely. The engine runs ordered passes: exact full-string match with word boundaries, then case-insensitive full-string, then component level, then token-based for name types, then partial match for addresses with city extraction. There is no separate recombination step. A split entity reassembles because each component was substituted at its own position in the text.

Twin reuse has a deliberate 60-second cross-continuation window. Files uploaded in a batch under different continuation IDs within a minute of each other stay consistent with one another. If someone tells you reuse is strictly scoped to a single continuation, that is not accurate.

Altitude rule: know it, do not say it

Everything in the two paragraphs above is internal. It is here so you can hold your own in a technical room and know when a question is out of bounds. It is not what you say to a prospect.

Our standing rule for prospect-facing capability answers: state the outcome, name the mechanism in one clause, say plainly what is not shipped, and stop. No algorithm names, no window sizes, no pipeline stages, no guard lists, no deployment topologies. The test: could a competitor rebuild a feature from what you just wrote? If yes, cut it back to the mechanism's name and keep the rest for the call.

This is not theoretical. In September 2026 a nine-question due-diligence reply ran roughly 1,550 words with the twin properties, hash algorithm, n-gram window size, decode guards and memory pipeline. Jason cut it: "You could almost take this whole email and reverse engineer our product." The corrected draft was half the length, answered every question, and kept the internals for the call.

4. Zero-knowledge, stated precisely

"Zero-knowledge" is a phrase reviewers will push on. Here is the accurate version, and it is strong enough that you never need to overstate it:

  • The detection, substitution and reversion all execute on the device.
  • The on-device core reaches PrivacyPal for exactly two things: a signed entitlement lease (default four-hour TTL) and sanitized audit receipts.
  • An audit receipt carries platform, timestamp and entity types. The prompt itself is stored as [processed on-device]. The content is not transmitted.
  • Enforcement is fail-closed on the prompt path. Encode failure, an expired session and an unreadable prompt all refuse the request. There is no passthrough mode.

That last bullet is worth its weight in a security review. Most competing architectures degrade to "allow" when their inspection path breaks. Ours refuses.

4. Private MCP

A proprietary Model Context Protocol runtime. It is a twin-translating proxy that mirrors the upstream tool's contract exactly, so the agent does not know the difference. The model sees only twins. The system of record sends and receives real values. The human sees the real answer.

The field story that sells it: a Max customer connected Claude straight to Salesforce. Because the desktop correctly twins what users type, Claude then queried and wrote Salesforce with twin values. Private MCP is the sanctioned route for any connector into a system holding PII or IP.

  • Built and shipping. OAuth 2.1 with PKCE, per-user grant vault, schema-typed encode, fail-closed guards.
  • Self-host is available now with a developer key. Pro and SDK customers with dev resources can run their own.
  • The hosted Private MCP plane is not stood up yet. Say "self-host available now, hosted plane being stood up." Do not sell the hosted plane as available.

5. Privacy Agents

On-device autonomous agents that reason about privacy, intent and risk in real time, inside the encode path. They adjust detection per intent: skip or force entity types, preserve an original, pass location through. Executions are persisted and alerts recorded. Built-in functions include Intent Detection, Prompt Injection Guard, Legal Enrichment and Financial Enrichment.

6. Private Memory, and the claims you may make

Memory that persists across AI tools, kept in twin space. The differentiated claim is simple and measured: remembered values never reach the provider.

Approved (cite "internal benchmark, 2026")

Memory in twin space does not degrade recall. In our benchmark, twin-space matched or beat plaintext.

Remembered values never reach the provider. Measured per value.

100% of our adversarial memory-poisoning cases were blocked, with clean abstention when no memory exists.

Memory Packs cut context cost about 56% versus replaying history, benchmark-measured.

Recall adds under 100ms. No LLM in the read path.

Erasure is mechanical and immediate. Crypto-shred is contract-tested, including mid-consolidation.

Not approved

Any bare accuracy percentage as a product claim. Ten self-authored cases is a regression gate, not a benchmark result.

Any head-to-head vendor comparison on memory. We have no common-benchmark basis.

Any generalization of the twin advantage beyond "matched or beat in our benchmark."

The reason this discipline exists: the memory-vendor field is burned by self-reported numbers. One competitor published 94% on a public benchmark and was independently measured at 49%. Our position is to be the vendor whose claims survive independent testing, and it costs us nothing because the mechanism claims are the differentiated ones anyway.

4. Accounting, the segment worth knowing cold

The accounting wedge is different from every other vertical, and it is the sharpest one we have. It is not framed as security. It is framed as consent.

The argument, in the buyer's own regulatory language

Under Section 7216, disclosing or using tax return information without the taxpayer's written consent is a criminal matter: a misdemeanor carrying up to $1,000 and a year. Section 6713 adds a $250 civil penalty per disclosure. A staff accountant pasting a K-1 into a public chatbot is a disclosure.

Style rule from the founder: write and say "Section 7216" and "Section 6713". Never the section-sign form. It reads as legal boilerplate to a practitioner audience.

Three more arguments this segment runs on:

  1. An AI policy is not AI governance. Drawn from a real prospect who observed that he "had an AI policy before he had governance."
  2. Busy season and offshore staff. Seasonal preparers and offshore teams are recurring Section 7216 disclosure decisions and third-party service provider questions. Max domain provisioning answers both.
  3. Your ledger is not training data. The firm's own IP is at risk alongside client identity: tax strategies refined over decades, engagement templates, pricing, client lists, workpaper methodology.

The Financial Twins Toggle is this segment's primary product message and, per a real customer call, "the first question every accountant will ask." Dollar amounts are twinned by default; financial twins can be switched off per user so money values pass through untouched. Names, SSNs, EINs, account numbers and dates keep full protection either way.

On point tools: Blue J and TaxGPT are good at what they were built for. We do not replace them and we make no claim to intercept their traffic. Module 07 of 09

MEDDIC and the proposal gate

This is the module the certification exam weights most heavily. MEDDIC is not paperwork. It is the discipline that stops you from spending six weeks on a deal that was never going to close, and at PrivacyPal it is wired into the system as a hard gate on whether a proposal can be assembled at all.

TL;DR
  • Six criteria. Each scored unknown (0), partial (1), confirmed (2). Total 0 to 12.
  • The gate: Identify Pain = confirmed AND Economic Buyer at least partial AND Champion at least partial AND score at least 7.
  • Failing the gate does not mean the deal is bad. It means you have not done the work yet.
  • Sending a proposal against the gate is recorded on the deal and tracked against win rate.

1. Where it comes from, and which variant we run

MEDDIC was created in the 1990s by Jack Napoli and Dick Dunkel at Parametric Technology Corporation, where it helped take annual sales from roughly $300M past $1B. Its value was never the acronym. It was giving a whole sales organization one shared language for what "qualified" means.

Two extensions exist and you should know them because buyers and other reps use the terms:

  • MEDDICC adds Competition: the alternatives, including doing nothing.
  • MEDDPICC adds Paper Process: legal, procurement, security paperwork, MSAs, DPAs.

PrivacyPal runs the six-criterion MEDDIC, scored 0 to 12, because that is what the gate is built on. Competition and paper process still matter enormously in our deals, they just live inside Decision Criteria and Decision Process rather than as their own letters. Do not report a "MEDDPICC score" on a PrivacyPal deal. There is no such field.

2. The six criteria, at PrivacyPal

M · Metrics

The quantified impact the buyer cares about. Not our metrics. Theirs. "340ms latency" is our metric. "We lose eleven analyst hours a week rewriting things by hand because AI is banned" is theirs.

StatusWhat it looks like
UnknownYou have talked about value in adjectives. Faster, safer, better.
PartialThe buyer named a metric but not a number, or a number you supplied and they nodded at.
ConfirmedThe buyer stated a number, in their own words, that they will be measured on. "Two weeks to onboard all users." "We are budgeting 40 hours a month of manual review."
Questions that get to Metrics
  • If AI were fully approved tomorrow, what would change that you could count?
  • How many people are affected by the current position, and what are they doing instead?
  • What did the last compliance incident or near-miss cost you, in hours or in dollars?
  • How would you know, ninety days after rollout, that this was worth doing?

E · Economic Buyer

The person who can say yes when everyone else says no, and whose budget it comes from. At an SME this is frequently the CEO, COO, managing partner or practice owner. Not the CISO.

StatusWhat it looks like
UnknownYou have a name from an org chart. You have never spoken to them and nobody has confirmed they hold the budget.
PartialIdentified and confirmed by someone inside the account, but you have not met them.
ConfirmedYou have been in a conversation with them, or they have visibly acted (approved a budget line, joined a call, forwarded a request).
The most common self-deception in this entire framework

A sponsor is not an economic buyer. Someone who says "I will take this to the board" is telling you they are not the economic buyer. Someone who says "I need to check with finance" has a constraint you have not mapped. Write down the actual name and how you know.

D · Decision Criteria

What they will judge us on. In our category this is nearly always some combination of: architecture verifiability, coverage across their actual AI surface list, endpoint burden, accuracy impact, audit evidence, deployment effort, and price. Your job is not just to learn the criteria. It is to shape them, so that the mechanism ladder and the on-device architecture are on the list before a competitor gets to write it.

Questions that get to Decision Criteria
  • If you brought three vendors to your risk committee, what would the comparison table's columns be?
  • Which of those columns is a hard requirement and which is a preference?
  • Who wrote the requirements, and when were they last updated?
  • What would make you rule someone out in the first ten minutes?

D · Decision Process

The steps and the timeline, including our Security Review stage. Who has to sign, in what order, by when, and what artifact each step needs.

StatusWhat it looks like
Unknown"They said they will get back to us."
PartialYou know the steps but not the dates, or the dates but not who owns them.
ConfirmedYou can name each step, its owner, its date, and what document it consumes. Including legal, procurement and security.

In regulated accounts this is where deals actually die. A security questionnaire, a vendor risk assessment, a DPA, an insurance certificate, and a board meeting that happens once a quarter. Find the quarterly meeting early or you will discover it in week nine.

I · Identify Pain

The concrete leakage or compliance pain. This is the only criterion that must be confirmed for the gate to open, and that is deliberate. Everything else in a PrivacyPal deal follows from a real, specific, named pain.

Not painPain

"Security is a priority for us.""An associate pasted a client engagement letter into ChatGPT in March. We found out in May."

Questions that get to Identify Pain

  • Walk me through the last time someone in the firm did something with AI that made you uncomfortable.

An internal advocate who sells on your behalf when you are not in the room. Three tests, all of which must be true: they have influence, they have a personal win if this happens, and they will actively sell it.

RoleTest

3. Scoring and the gate

Six criteria in, one binary decision out. The gate is arithmetic, not judgement.

5. How MEDDIC gets into the system

You do not fill in a spreadsheet. Post-call extraction reads the call recording and writes the six per-criterion statuses, the total score and the gate result onto the deal, alongside pains, objections, sentiment trajectory, buying signals and stakeholders. It also writes an account brain entry with verbatim quotes and a rep debrief.

Two consequences you should internalize:

  1. What you get on the call is what gets scored. If you never asked who signs, the extraction cannot invent an economic buyer. Vague calls produce low scores mechanically.
  2. The verbatim quote matters more than your summary. Get the buyer to say the number out loud. A confirmed metric is a sentence in their voice, and the system captures it.
How to use MEDDIC on a live call without sounding like a robot

Never work the acronym in order. Work it as a checklist after the call, then design the next call around the two weakest criteria. The best reps end every call by closing exactly one gap: "Before we wrap, who else would need to be comfortable with this before it happens?" That single question moves Economic Buyer and Decision Process at once.

4. "Send me everything in writing"

This is the most dangerous request you will get, because it feels like progress. A written answer is permanent, forwardable, and quotable back at you. The failure mode is real: a nine-question reply that ran roughly 1,550 words with algorithm names, window sizes, guard lists and pipeline stages, which the founder cut with "You could almost take this whole email and reverse engineer our product."

The written answer, correct altitude

"Sensitive values never reach the provider. They are replaced on the device with synthetic equivalents before the request leaves, and restored in the response. Document attachments on ChatGPT and Gemini are currently forwarded unchanged; that work is queued. Happy to walk your architect through the detail on a call."

The written answer that costs us

Anything naming the detection algorithms, the n-gram window, the number and order of reversion passes, the guard list, the lease cadence, or the deployment topology. Those belong on a call, in a demo, or in a controlled document such as the Cloud Technical Whitepaper.

Run this over any email, deck, one-pager or proposal you write for a prospect.

  2. Did I use a numbered framework?
  3. Is the call to action a simple verb?
  4. Did I name the mechanism that makes the claim true?
  5. Did I scan for spaced em dashes and remove every one?

On the em dash rule, since candidates always ask

The objection you must be able to answer in your sleep

"WitnessAI restores data too, so what is the difference?"

Three-part answer:

  1. Tokens carry no semantics. Their surrogates preserve referential integrity, so the model knows two mentions are the same thing. It does not know what kind of thing, in what industry, at what scale. The reasoning degrades even though the round trip works.
  2. Deterministic tokens leak patterns. If the same input always produces the same surrogate, the surrogate becomes a stable identifier across sessions and documents.
  3. Their own documentation steers code and IP to internal models. That is a vendor telling you their mechanism is not sufficient for the highest-value content. Ask the prospect to read that page.

Then move to architecture: their gateway is fed by the customer's network path. Ask what happens to the coding agent running locally on an engineer's laptop.
