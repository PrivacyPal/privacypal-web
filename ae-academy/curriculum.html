Module 01 of 07

The market and the wedge

Before you can sell PrivacyPal you have to explain, in ninety seconds, why a competent security leader is blocking the most valuable productivity tool their company has ever had. That explanation is the sale.

TL;DR
  • Every company is running AI whether or not it approved AI. The question is not adoption, it is governance.
  • Security teams have three bad options today: ban it, write a policy nobody enforces, or look away.
  • Every vendor in this category sits on a mechanism ladder. Every rung above "coach" costs the buyer accuracy. One mechanism costs nothing.
  • Our wedge is not fear. It is turning the person who blocks AI into the person who ships it.

1. What actually changed

Ten years of data-loss prevention was built on one assumption: sensitive data leaves the company as a file, through a channel you can inspect. Email attachments. USB drives. Uploads to a known host. DLP watched the channels.

Generative AI broke that twice over. The payload became a prompt, free text typed by a human mid-thought, with no file, no classification label, nothing to pattern-match. And the channel became every channel: a browser tab, a desktop app, an IDE, a coding agent on a laptop, an agent talking to another agent.

So the security leader is looking at a category of exposure their stack was never built to see. That is the emotional state you walk into. Not "we do not care about AI." It is "we cannot see it, so we said no, and now the business hates us."

Protection with no accuracy tax

Walk a technical buyer up this ladder and let them place their current tooling on it themselves. They land on rung 3 or 4, and they already know rungs 3 and 4 are why nobody uses the tool. You never have to attack a competitor. The ladder does it.

The distinction that wins the technical argument

Tokenization preserves referential integrity. Substitution preserves meaning. Replace "Acme Corp" with ENTITY_7741 and the model can tell two mentions are the same thing. It cannot tell what kind of thing, in what industry, at what scale. Ask a model to draft a credit memo about ENTITY_7741 earning NUM_0032 and you get sludge. Ask it about a mid-market industrial distributor earning $14.2M and you get a credit memo. That is the accuracy tax, and it is why redaction-era tools sit unused after the pilot.

Module 02 of 07

The mechanism: Privacy Twins on device

PrivacyPal is a mechanism-first company. You do not get to make a claim without naming the machinery that makes it true. This module is the machinery.

TL;DR
  • Privacy Twins are statistically accurate synthetic values substituted for real ones before a prompt leaves the device, and reverted in the response.
  • The whole pipeline runs on the endpoint. PrivacyPal never sees unencrypted customer data.
  • Five stages: detect, classify, synthesize, map, revert.
  • The prompt path is fail-closed. There is no passthrough mode.

1. The journey of one prompt

One prompt, start to finish. Everything inside the panel happens on the endpoint.

2. The five stages

StageWhat happens
1 · DetectionMulti-layer sensitive-data detection: named entity recognition, pattern matching, and component splitting, so a full name, a street address or an account number is broken into its parts.
2 · ClassificationThe detected entity is categorized. This drives which kind of twin gets generated.
3 · SynthesisA statistically faithful synthetic value is generated for that entity type. Model-based, not a random string.
4 · MappingThe real-to-twin mapping is stored in the encrypted vault on the device. It is the only place the relationship exists and it never leaves the boundary.
5 · ReversionOn the response, twins are swapped back to real values. Multi-turn conversations stay coherent across the whole thread.
Answer plus mechanism, then stop

You need to understand the pipeline. You do not recite it. Our rule for any capability question from a prospect: state the outcome, name the mechanism in one clause, and stop. No algorithm names, no window sizes, no internal stage lists.

The test: could a competitor rebuild a feature from what you just said or wrote? If yes, cut back to the mechanism's name and keep the rest for the technical call. Written answers are permanent, forwardable and quotable, so this matters most in email.

3. Where interception happens

SurfaceHow it works
DesktopWindows and macOSA tray application that intercepts AI traffic at the network layer on the device. This covers the browser too, so ChatGPT in a Chrome tab and the ChatGPT desktop app are both governed by one install. Nothing for the user to learn, nothing to switch on per app.

4. Zero-knowledge, stated precisely

Reviewers will push on the phrase. Here is the accurate version, and it is strong enough that you never need to overstate it:

  • Detection, substitution and reversion all execute on the device.
  • The on-device core reaches PrivacyPal for exactly two things: a signed entitlement lease, and sanitized audit receipts.
  • An audit receipt carries platform, timestamp and entity types. The prompt itself is recorded as processed on-device. The content is never transmitted.
  • Enforcement is fail-closed on the prompt path. Encode failure, an expired session and an unreadable prompt all refuse the request. There is no passthrough mode.

That last point is worth its weight in a security review. Most competing architectures degrade to "allow" when their inspection path breaks. Ours refuses.

Module 06 of 07

MEDDIC and the proposal gate

The exam weights this module most heavily. MEDDIC is not paperwork. It is the discipline that stops you spending six weeks on a deal that was never going to close, and here it is wired in as a hard gate on whether a proposal can be assembled at all.

TL;DR
  • Six criteria, each scored unknown 0, partial 1, confirmed 2. Total 0 to 12.
  • The gate: Identify Pain confirmed AND Economic Buyer at least partial AND Champion at least partial AND score at least 7.
  • A passing score is not a passing gate. All four conditions are independent.
  • Failing the gate does not mean the deal is bad. It means you have not done the work yet.

1. Where it comes from

Created in the 1990s by Jack Napoli and Dick Dunkel at Parametric Technology Corporation, where it helped take annual sales from roughly $300M past $1B. Its value was never the acronym, it was giving a whole sales organization one shared language for what "qualified" means.

You will hear two extensions: MEDDICC adds Competition, MEDDPICC adds Paper Process. We run the six-criterion MEDDIC, because that is what the gate is built on. Competition and paperwork live inside Decision Criteria and Decision Process.

2. The six criteria

M · Metrics

The quantified impact the buyer cares about. "340ms" is our metric. "We lose eleven analyst hours a week because AI is banned" is theirs. Confirmed means they stated a number, in their own words, that they are measured on.

Ask: If AI were approved tomorrow, what would change that you could count?

E · Economic Buyer

The person who can say yes when everyone else says no, and whose budget it comes from. At an SME that is frequently the CEO, COO, managing partner or practice owner, not the CISO. Confirmed means you have been in a conversation with them, or they have visibly acted.

The most common self-deception in the framework

A sponsor is not an economic buyer. Someone who says "I'll take this to the board" is telling you they are not the economic buyer. Someone who says "I need to check with finance" has a constraint you have not mapped. Write down the actual name and how you know.

D · Decision Criteria

What they will judge us on: architecture verifiability, coverage, endpoint burden, accuracy impact, audit evidence, deployment effort, price. Your job is not only to learn the criteria, it is to shape them, so the mechanism ladder and the on-device architecture are on the list before a competitor writes it.

Ask: If you brought three vendors to your risk committee, what would the comparison columns be? Which are hard requirements and which are preferences?

D · Decision Process

The steps, the owners and the dates, including our Security Review stage. In regulated accounts this is where deals die: a questionnaire, a vendor risk assessment, a DPA, and a board that meets once a quarter. Find the quarterly meeting early or you will discover it in week nine.

I · Identify Pain

The concrete leakage or compliance pain. This is the only criterion that must be confirmed for the gate to open, and that is deliberate: everything else in a PrivacyPal deal follows from a real, specific, named pain.

Not painPain
"We're interested in AI governance.""Our examiner asked what controls we have on generative AI and we didn't have an answer."
"Security is a priority for us.""An associate pasted a client engagement letter into ChatGPT in March. We found out in May."
"We're exploring options.""Three enterprise prospects put our AI feature through security review and two stalled."

Ask: What made you take this call this month rather than six months ago? What happens to you personally if this is still unresolved at year end?

C · Champion

An internal advocate who sells on your behalf when you are not in the room. Three tests, all of which must be true: they have influence, they have a personal win, and they will actively sell it.

A coach gives you information but has no influence and no stake. A sponsor has influence and wants it to happen, but will not spend political capital. A champion has done something: booked the internal meeting, argued for you in a room you were not in.

Test a champion by asking them to do something. Get me thirty minutes with the managing partner. A champion does it. A sponsor explains why now is not the right time.

3. Scoring and the gate

Six criteria in, one binary decision out. The gate is arithmetic, not judgement.

4. The trap, worked

A deal can pass with two criteria still partial. Now the one that fools people, a 900-person regional bank:

The score clears the threshold. The gate fails on two conditions: Identify Pain is only partial when it must be confirmed, and the Economic Buyer is unknown when it must be at least partial. This is exactly the deal that feels good, has a big logo, and burns a quarter.

The work list is obvious once you score it: find the real event behind "we're worried", and get confirmation of who signs. Two conversations, not two months.

Using it on a live call without sounding like a robot

Never work the acronym in order. Work it as a checklist after the call, then design the next call around the two weakest criteria. The best reps close exactly one gap at the end of every call: "Before we wrap, who else would need to be comfortable with this before it happens?" That single question moves Economic Buyer and Decision Process at once.

  • Six criteria, 0 / 1 / 2 each, total 0 to 12.
  • The gate, word for word: Identify Pain confirmed AND Economic Buyer at least partial AND Champion at least partial AND score at least 7.
  • A passing score is not a passing gate.
  • Champion test: influence, personal win, and demonstrated action. Ask them to do something.
  • We run the six-criterion MEDDIC, not MEDDPICC.
Module 07 of 07

Discovery, objections and closing

Three rooms. In the first you shut up and take notes. In the second you show one thing. In the third they push back, and everything you said in the first two gets checked.

TL;DR
  • Discovery has one job: a confirmed pain in the buyer's own words.
  • A demo is not a tour. Show one before-and-after on data that looks like theirs.
  • Every objection maps to one of ten codes. Code it, answer it, log it.
  • Never attack a competitor. Walk them up the ladder and let them place the other vendor on it.

1. Discovery, in four movements

  1. Situation. Where did they land on AI, and who made that call?
  2. The event. What happened, on a date, that put this on the agenda?
  3. The cost of the current position. What is the ban, the policy or the silence costing, in hours, dollars or stalled deals?
  4. The path. What has to be true for this to happen, and who has to be comfortable?
If you are talking to the technical buyer
  • What is your position on generative AI today, and how is it enforced?
  • If I asked you to produce evidence of AI usage for last month, what could you produce?
  • What happens on an unmanaged or personal device today?
  • What would you need to see architecturally before you would recommend a yes?
If you are talking to the business owner
  • How much of your team is using AI today, with or without permission?
  • Who told you no, and what would it take for them to say yes?
  • Is there a date on the calendar, an audit, a renewal, a busy season, this has to be ahead of?
  • If this were solved, what is the first thing you would let people do?
If you are talking to a builder
  • Where in your product does customer data reach a model?
  • How many enterprise deals are sitting in security review because of it?
  • What does adding a model provider to your subprocessor list cost you?

2. The demo, in five beats

  1. Type a realistic prompt with sensitive values from their world. Recognition matters more than polish.
  2. Show what the provider actually received. Point at the twins.
  3. Show the answer coming back, reverted and useful. This kills the accuracy objection before it is raised.
  4. Show the audit record. This is the artifact a compliance buyer needs and no policy can produce.
  5. Stop. Ask what they want to see next. What they ask for is your Decision Criteria.

3. The Security Review

Expect a questionnaire, a call with an architect, and follow-up in writing. Two rules carry you through it:

  • Answer plus mechanism, then stop. Outcome first, mechanism in one clause, no internals. A written answer is permanent and forwardable.
  • If you do not know, say so and come back. Nobody expects a rep to be the architect. They do expect you not to guess.

Your three strongest architectural answers: everything happens on the device; the prompt path is fail-closed, so it refuses rather than degrading to allow; there is an audit record per prompt. If the requirement is self-hosting, that is Cloud, and say so early rather than fighting it.

The competitive objection to know cold

"WitnessAI restores data too, so what's the difference?"

  1. Tokens carry no semantics. Their surrogates preserve referential integrity, so the model knows two mentions are the same thing. It does not know what kind of thing, in what industry, at what scale. The reasoning degrades even though the round trip works.
  2. Deterministic tokens leak patterns. If the same input always produces the same surrogate, that surrogate becomes a stable identifier across sessions and documents.
  3. Their own documentation steers code and IP to internal models. That is a vendor telling you their mechanism is not sufficient for the highest-value content.

Then move to architecture: their gateway is fed by the customer's network path. Ask what happens to the coding agent running locally on an engineer's laptop.

Unique to us in this set: on-device end to end with no vendor-cloud transit, endpoint data-at-rest discovery, Private MCP, and no seat floor.

7. How we behave

Five rules that apply from day one
  1. Never fabricate. No source, no fact. Customer names, seat counts, metrics, dates, all of it.
  2. Mechanism first. No claim without the machinery behind it.
  3. A human at every decision point. The automation drafts, you decide.
  4. If you do not know, go and find out. Ten seconds of humility beats a month of cleanup.

8. What certification means

Passing the exam makes you a PrivacyPal Certified Sales Professional. In practice it means we have verified you can do four things:

  1. Explain the mechanism to a technical buyer without reading from anything.
  2. Size and structure a deal correctly against the price book.
  3. Qualify against MEDDIC and say plainly whether the gate is open.
  4. Hold your ground under pressure without overclaiming.

The exam is six sections, about half an hour. Long-form answers are read by a person and we interview on them, so write what you actually think rather than what you think we want to read.
