Brand pack

The short version

Six rules cover almost every case. The rest of this page explains them and gives you the files.

  1. The penguin is the emoji. The Twemoji 🐧, unaltered and credited. Use the files in assets/brand; never redraw, restyle or recolor it.
  2. Give it room. Clear space equals the penguin's height. Below 120px wide, use the penguin alone.
  3. Navy, teal, gold. Gold is a slight accent: about 10% of a surface at most, and never text on white.
  4. Teal acts on light, gold acts on navy. The primary button is teal on light grounds, gold only on navy or dark photography.
  5. One product, three packs. PrivacyPal, with Personal (free), Family and Max switching on inside the same app.
  6. Say it plainly. Sentence case, no em dashes, say what PrivacyPal does, and use only numbers with a source.
01 · Why a penguin

Calm, loyal and dressed for work.

PrivacyPal stands between your data and every AI. The character in front of it had to look calm under pressure, trustworthy in a boardroom and friendly at a kitchen table. A penguin is all three.

  1. 01It already wears the uniform.

    Dark and white, buttoned up and composed. It belongs in a bank's security review and on a nine-year-old's tablet, and it looks the same in both.

  2. 02It guards what matters.

    An emperor penguin keeps its egg on its feet through the depth of the Antarctic winter. Quiet, patient protection of the thing you care about is the job.

  3. 03It is already on every keyboard.

    The mark is the penguin emoji itself, so anyone can type our logo. In a post, a bio or a chat, 🐧 PrivacyPal says who we are in one character and a name.

Writing it out

Put the penguin before the name, with one space, and keep to one penguin per line. On PrivacyPal pages, in Discord and on the X website, the typed 🐧 is our mark exactly; elsewhere the device draws its own.

05 · Misuse

Eight ways to break the logo.

Each of these has happened to a logo somewhere. The fix is almost always the same: use the right file from assets/brand, untouched.

RecoloredNever recolor the penguin or the wordmark, even to match a campaign. The penguin keeps Twemoji's colors.
RestyledNever flip, tilt, outline, shadow or animate the penguin, and never redraw or trace it.
🐧PrivacyPal
Another vendor's penguinNever use Apple's, Google's, Microsoft's or any other vendor's emoji artwork, and never build the logo from an emoji and a typeface.
Gold tileOn gold, the beak and feet disappear. The app icon and avatar use the white tile.
StretchedNever stretch or squash it. Scale by width with the height on auto.
FilteredNever whiten it with a filter: it erases the penguin. On navy, use logo-white.svg.
Busy photoOnly over a dark, calm area of a photo. Never across faces, detail or bright color.
LinuxHeadless agent
Another penguin's jobLinux has its own penguin. Keep ours apart: the Linux tile uses the >_ glyph.

The gold rules

Gold is a slight accent, never a theme.

  1. About 10% of any surface, at most. One gold moment per section is usually right.
  2. Where gold belongs: the penguin's own beak and feet, eyebrow dots, a highlighter behind one or two words, an underline under a key number, small badges, icon accents, focus rings, the active tab or toggle, thin rules, and the stat number on a navy band.
  3. Gold as text only on navy or dark photography (8.5:1 on navy). On white, use Gold Ink for a small label if it must be gold-family.
  4. The gold button lives on navy. On navy or a dark photo, the primary button may be gold with navy text. On white and light grounds, the primary button stays teal.
  5. Never gold with coral in the same element. Never a gold gradient on text.

08 · Photography

Real people, warm light, nothing staged.

Our photographs look like moments someone was lucky to catch: people at home, at work and on their own, in natural light. The product shows the protection; the photo shows the life it protects.

One grade across every image. Warm highlights, natural skin, deep navy shadows and a touch of gold in the light, so a living room and a glass-walled office look like the same day.

01

Real people, real places

Nobody poses for the camera. Lived-in rooms, real offices, natural expressions caught mid-moment.

02

Warm, natural light

Golden hour or soft window light, shallow depth of field, a cinematic feel.

03

Screens stay unreadable

Any screen in shot is out of focus or angled away. Nothing on it can be read.

04

Room for the words

Landscape heroes keep a calm, shadowed third on the left for the headline.

05

No security clichés

No padlocks, shields, glowing holograms or blue code overlays. The interface cards show the protection.

06

Work looks like home

Office scenes take the same grade: warm desk light, navy dusk outside, people at ease.

10 · Voice and naming

The Operator.

We write like a calm expert briefing one person: the point first, the mechanism named, and a clear next step. Never louder than the facts.

01

Lead with the point

The answer comes first, the detail after. If a reader stops after one line, they still know what we do.

02

Name the mechanism

Say how it works in one clause: on the device, before the AI provider, with Privacy Twins.

03

Talk to one person

Second person, sentence case, short headlines. Buttons are verbs: Download free.

04

Say what it does

Describe what PrivacyPal does, never what it lacks. Every number comes from a source.

Core line

We protect your data from AI, at home and at work.

Proof line

The same technology that protects 140+ companies and global banks.

Mechanism

Protection runs on the device, before a prompt or file reaches an AI provider. Sensitive details are swapped for Privacy Twins, realistic stand-ins, so the AI still works and the real data never reaches the AI provider.

There is one product, PrivacyPal, and one download. Everything a person can buy or be given is a pack that switches on inside the same app.

  • History. Earlier product names stay only in historical records (blog posts, news releases, past event pages) and in legal documents.
